## Key ideas

- **Exact answers only**: no calculator, no rounding ($\tfrac13$ is not $0.33$).
- Factor before expanding. Quadratic formula $x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$; discriminant $D=b^2-4ac$ gives the number of real roots.
- For $x^2+px+q$ with roots $r,s$: $r+s=-p$ and $rs=q$.
- **Inequalities**: multiplying or dividing by a negative number flips the sign; for $f(x)<g(x)$, solve $f=g$, then test intervals.
- **Absolute value**: $|x-a|<b \iff a-b<x<a+b$.
- **Composition** $(f\circ g)(x)=f(g(x))$; **inverse**: swap $x$ and $y$ and solve; the domain of $f^{-1}$ is the range of $f$.
- **Transformations** of $y=f(x)$: $f(x-a)$ shifts right, $f(x)+b$ shifts up, $af(x)$ stretches vertically, $f(bx)$ compresses horizontally.
- Systems $ax+by=c,\ dx+ey=f$: eliminate a variable.

## Common traps

- Squaring both sides and keeping an extraneous solution — always substitute back.
- Dividing by an expression that can be zero (losing a solution).
- Forgetting domain restrictions: $\sqrt{\cdot}\ge 0$ inside, $\ln$ needs positive input, no division by zero.
