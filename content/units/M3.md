## Key ideas

- Standard derivatives: $(x^a)'=ax^{a-1}$, $(e^x)'=e^x$, $(a^x)'=a^x\ln a$, $(\ln x)'=\frac1x$, $(\sin x)'=\cos x$, $(\cos x)'=-\sin x$, $(\tan x)'=\frac{1}{\cos^2x}$.
- **Product** $(fg)'=f'g+fg'$, **quotient** $\left(\frac fg\right)'=\frac{f'g-fg'}{g^2}$, **chain** $(f(g(x)))'=f'(g(x))\,g'(x)$.
- **Implicit differentiation**: differentiate both sides with respect to $x$, treating $y$ as $y(x)$ (so $(y^2)'=2y\,y'$), then solve for $y'$.
- **Tangent line** at $x=a$: $y=f(a)+f'(a)(x-a)$. **Normal line**: slope $-1/f'(a)$.
- The second derivative measures concavity.

## Common traps

- Forgetting the inner derivative in the chain rule.
- Sign errors in the quotient rule (it is $f'g-fg'$, in that order).
- Using the derivative value instead of the function value as the point on the tangent line.
