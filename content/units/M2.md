## Key ideas

- $\lim_{x\to a} f(x)=L$ means $f(x)$ gets arbitrarily close to $L$ as $x$ approaches $a$ (from both sides).
- For continuous standard functions, substitute. For $\frac00$: factor and cancel, or rationalise.
- **One-sided limits** must agree for the two-sided limit to exist (watch absolute values and piecewise functions).
- **Limits at infinity** of rational functions: compare highest powers. Equal degree → ratio of leading coefficients; numerator lower → 0.
- **Asymptotes**: vertical where the denominator is 0 and the numerator is not; horizontal from the limit at ±∞.
- Standard limits: $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1$, exponentials beat polynomials, polynomials beat logarithms.
- $f$ is **continuous** at $a$ if $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=f(a)$. A removable discontinuity is a "hole" you can fill.

## Common traps

- Concluding a limit does not exist just because $f(a)$ is undefined.
- Cancelling a factor and forgetting the original function is still undefined at that point.
- Mixing up the behaviour at $+\infty$ and $-\infty$ (e.g. $e^x$).
