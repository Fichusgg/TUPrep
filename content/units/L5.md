## Key ideas

- **"p only if q"** means $p\to q$ (q is necessary for p). **"p if q"** means $q\to p$. **"p unless q"** means $\neg q\to p$, i.e. $p\lor q$.
- **Necessary** vs **sufficient**: "A is sufficient for B" is $A\to B$; "A is necessary for B" is $B\to A$.
- **Knights and knaves**: a statement $S$ made by $X$ satisfies $X\text{ is a knight}\leftrightarrow S$. Write that biconditional for each speaker and solve. Nobody can say "I am a knave".
- **Constraint puzzles**: draw the grid/line of slots first, place the most restrictive clue, then branch on small case splits and eliminate contradictions.
- "Must be true" means in *every* consistent arrangement. "Could be true" means in *at least one*.
- Read dense statements twice: underline every connective and every quantifier word (all, some, none, only, at least, exactly).

## Common traps

- Turning "only if" around (it is the *opposite* direction from "if").
- Treating "unless" as "if not … then not …" in both directions — it is one-directional.
- Stopping after finding one arrangement when the question asks what *must* be true.
- In knights-and-knaves, forgetting that a knave's compound statement is false as a whole (so "A and B" by a knave means at least one part is false).
