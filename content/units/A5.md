## Key ideas

- **Multiplication principle**: independent choices multiply. **Addition principle**: disjoint cases add.
- Order matters and no repetition: $n!/(n-k)!$. Order does not matter: $\binom{n}{k}$. With repetition and order: $n^k$.
- **Inclusion–exclusion**: $|A\cup B| = |A|+|B|-|A\cap B|$ (and the three-set version).
- **Complement counting**: "at least one" = total − "none".
- **Pigeonhole**: with $n$ boxes and more than $n$ objects, some box has at least two.
- Optimisation puzzles: first find *any* valid solution, then argue why nothing better exists (a lower bound). Greedy choices are fast but not always optimal — test them on small cases.

## Common traps

- Double counting when cases overlap.
- Using combinations when order matters (or the reverse).
- Assuming a greedy answer is optimal without checking a counter-example.
